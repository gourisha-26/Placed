Placed - usability test plan

Five moderated tasks, with the script to read and the questions to ask. Each task is a separate scenario with its own starting point in the Figma prototype.

5
tasks
16
participants
50min
per session

Who we test with

College students - primary audience Final-year students who are applying, or about to. They do all five tasks. The findings from this group decide what changes.
Placement cell and faculty - expert review They know the process end to end. They check whether the app matches how drives actually run, not whether it's easy to use.

Opening script

Read this out at the start. Keep it close to the wording - changing it between sessions changes the results.

Read this out

Thanks for making time - this will take about 50 minutes.

I'm testing a design for a placement tracking app called Placed. I'm not testing you, and I didn't build this to have it praised. I need to find where it breaks. So there's no wrong answer here, and if something confuses you, that's exactly what I need to hear.

Please think aloud as you go. Say what you're looking at, what you expect to happen, and what you're about to tap - even if it turns out to be wrong.

This is a prototype, so only some parts respond. If you tap something and nothing happens, just tell me what you were trying to do and I'll move us along.

Is it okay if I record the screen and the audio? It stays with me and is only used for this project.

Before we open anything - walk me through how you keep track of placement applications right now.

Consent and recording Get a clear yes before you start recording anything.
Warm-up - 3 minutes The last line above. Capture their current method before showing any screen.
Open each task at its own start point Five separate flows in the Figma play menu. Don't run them as one story.
Stay quiet while they work Only prompt if they've been stuck 30 seconds. Note it as assisted when you do.
Questions, then an ease rating Ask the task's questions, then rate ease 1-7, before moving on.
Say this between every task

That's the end of that one. The next task is a separate situation - treat it as a fresh day. You'll see some of the same companies at a different stage, and that's on purpose, not a mistake.

The five tasks

1 · Connect your inboxAll participants · about 8 minutes

First run. Tests whether students see why a tracked inbox matters - and how many refuse or skip.

Read this out

You've just downloaded Placed for the first time. Nothing is set up yet.

Set Placed up, and connect the email you actually use to apply for jobs - not necessarily your college ID.
Onboarding→Which inbox→Connect→Confirmed→Passport?→Home
They chooseWhat happensEnds at
GmailConsent, then 3 placement emails already foundHome, tracking on
Another emailProvider list, Outlook connectedHome, tracking on
More than oneConnect first, then add secondHome, tracking on
SkipShown what stops workingHome, tracking off
Criteria to measure
How many connect an inbox - log refusals separately
Passport filled now, or skipped
Which branch they take, and how long they hesitate
Passes if 12 of 16 connect an inbox unaided, and every participant can say what access they just gave.
Ask after the task
1
In your own words, what did you just give the app access to?
2
Would you do this with your real email? If not, what's stopping you?
3
It said it found 3 placement emails. What did that tell you?
4
If they skipped - what made you skip that step?
Watch whether “3 placement emails found” registers. It's the only moment the app proves its worth instead of describing it.
2 · Capture your first jobAll participants · about 10 minutes · no resumes yet

The core loop, run cold. Tests whether the floating widget is findable and whether flagged fields get noticed.

Read this out

You're on your phone between classes, scrolling for openings. You haven't added any resumes to the app yet.

You've found a job posting you're interested in. Get it into the app.
Explainer→Chrome→Search→Posting→Reading→Review→Journey
Criteria to measure
Time to find the capture button, and wrong taps first
Is the flagged Deadline corrected before saving?
Do they act on the “no resumes yet” prompt, or ignore it?
Passes if 13 of 16 capture the job unaided in under 90 seconds, with the flagged deadline corrected.
Ask after the task
1
Where did you expect the capture button to be?
2
Could you use that on any website, or only inside Placed?
3
Was anything on the review screen wrong, or missing?
4
What would you do next with this job?
3 · Apply and update the statusStudents with 2+ resumes · about 12 minutes

Skip anyone with no resumes - the choice wouldn't mean anything. Gmail is connected here, so tracking works.

Read this out

You saved this job a few days ago and haven't applied yet. You have five resume versions in the app, and about twenty minutes free right now.

Open the job you've saved but not applied to, decide which resume to send, apply - then make sure the app knows.
Journey→Job detail→Compare 4→Portal→Did you apply?→Applied
Criteria to measure
Accept the suggestion, or open the picker - the headline number
Can they say why V5 was suggested?
Does the job get marked as applied without prompting?
Passes if 8 of 10 reach Applied status without being reminded to update the app.
Ask after the task
1
Why did you send that resume and not one of the others?
2
The app suggested one. Why do you think it picked that one?
3
After you submitted on the portal - how does the app know you applied?
4
If you'd closed the app right after applying, what would have happened?
After “Submit application”, say nothing at all. Silence is the only honest read of the return prompt - the weakest link in the product.
4 · Trust an automatic updateAll participants · about 8 minutes

A status changed on its own. Tests whether students check automated claims or take them on faith.

Read this out

It's a normal evening. You applied to a few companies last week. Your phone buzzes.

A notification says Adobe shortlisted you. Open it, and decide whether you believe it.
Lock screen→Job detail→Why this changed→Kept/Reverted

Adobe wrote “moving your profile forward for review.” Placed read that as Shortlisted. The screen shows the source, the exact wording, and what the app did - without arguing either way.

Criteria to measure
Do they open “How did we know?” at all?
Accept or reject, once they've read the wording
How long they spend on the quoted sentence
Passes if 12 of 16 read the source wording before accepting or rejecting the change.
Ask after the task
1
Do you believe that status is right? What made you decide?
2
What did Adobe actually say, in your own words?
3
If this had been wrong, what would have gone wrong for you?
4
Should the app change the status on its own, or ask you first?
Watch for any pause at the quoted sentence. If nobody hesitates, automatic status changes are being trusted blindly.
5 · Resolve a schedule clashAll participants · about 10 minutes

Two commitments overlap. Tests how they prioritise, and whether they trust a message the app wrote.

Read this out

It's Tuesday morning. You've got a few interviews lined up this week. Open the app the way you normally would.

Something's wrong on Friday. Sort it out.
Home alert→Friday 19→Compare→Draft→Requested
DeloitteInfosys
Time11:00 - 11:4511:30 - 13:00
Round3 of 3 - final1 of 4
FormatGoogle MeetProctored, on campus
Set byRecruiterPlacement cell
Criteria to measure
Which one they move, and the reason they give
Is the drafted email sent as-is, edited, or distrusted?
Does anyone want to keep both, or drop one? That path isn't built
Passes if 10 of 12 pick one interview and give a reason based on the round or who set it.
Ask after the task
1
Why that interview and not the other one?
2
Would you send that email as written? What would you change?
3
Did you spot the clash yourself, or did the app tell you?
4
What would you have done if the app hadn't flagged it?
Watch whether the overlap is read off the calendar, or only understood once they reach the text.

What to record

Per task, never averaged across tasks. A bad Task 3 score and a bad Task 1 score mean completely different things.

QuotesEspecially at consent, the return prompt, and the confidence meter

Closing script

Read this out

That's all the tasks - thank you. Just a few last questions about the app as a whole.

1
In one sentence, what is this app for?
2
What would make you stop using it in week two?
3
Which part actually worked for you? Which part felt like extra work?
4
What did the colours tell you, if anything?
5
Would you connect your real inbox? What would have to be true first?

Weighting the findings

Structural - could change the IA
  • Task 1 - consent refusal, passport skipping
  • Task 3 - the return prompt
  • Task 5 - whether prioritisation help is wanted
Surface - copy and affordance
  • Task 2 - finding the widget, noticing flags
  • Task 4 - how the confidence note is worded
What this prototype can't test
No real typing - fields are pre-filled and advance on a tap. Capture, OCR and the portal are simulated. Screens are static, so counts don't respond to what participants do. Colour carries meaning throughout, and colour-vision deficiency isn't covered here - run that separately.
