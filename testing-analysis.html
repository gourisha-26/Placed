Overview

I tested placED. with students through five realistic placement situations. The goal was to see whether students could understand and use the main features without extra explanation.

AreaDetails
Testing methodModerated usability testing
Tasks5 realistic placement tasks
Recorded sessions analysed12 recorded screen sessions
What I observedNavigation, hesitation, errors, skipped information and user comments

Who I tested the session with

College students - primary audience

Final-year students who are applying, or about to. They do all five tasks. The findings from this group decide what changes.

Placement cell and faculty - expert review

They know the process end to end. They check whether the app matches how drives actually run, not whether it's easy to use.

Post Test questions I asked

  • Which part actually worked for you? Which part felt like extra work?
  • Would you connect your real inbox? What would have to be true first?
  • Should the app change the status on its own, or ask you first?

What I wanted to learn

  • Can users set up placED. and connect their placement email?
  • Can users capture a job posting easily?
  • Can users choose the right resume and complete an application?
  • Do users trust an automatically detected placement update?
  • Can users resolve a schedule clash and review the message before it is sent?

Timing note: some recordings ended before every task was completed. Where a reliable end time was not available, the timing should be marked as “Not captured” rather than estimated.

The five tasks

Task 1

Connect your placement inbox

“Connect the email you actually use to apply for jobs.”
What I wanted to learnCan users understand the email setup and move through onboarding without confusion?
Flow Welcome→Choose inbox→ Gmail consent→Connected→ Student Passport→Home
V1 screens
TimeUse observed timings only. Completed flows varied; longer flows included extra Student Passport interaction.
What I observed
  • Most users found Student Passport confusing.
  • The previous screen did not clearly explain what Student Passport was or why it was needed.
  • Many users skipped “Fill it in now · 2 min” because they wanted to explore the app first.
  • 4 users selected “I use more than one” for placement emails.
Error / frictionStudent Passport was unclear, and completing it felt like extra setup before users had seen the value of the product.
Key insightUsers wanted to see the value of placED. before putting effort into setting it up.
Design changeMove Student Passport away from the first setup step and introduce it when its benefit is clear.

Task 2

Capture a job

“You’ve found a job application posting. Get it into the app.”
What I wanted to learnCan users find and use the job-capture feature without help?
Flow Home→How capture works→ Chrome→Search→ Job posting→Capture→ Review→Journey
V1 screens
TimeUse observed timings only. Users spent noticeable time in the capture explainer before reaching the job.
What I observed
  • Users clicked “Capture this job” while still on the tutorial screen.
  • This made the interaction confusing because the tutorial and the real action looked too closely related.
  • The “How capture works” section contained too much text.
Error / frictionUsers treated the tutorial CTA as the real capture action, and the amount of text slowed understanding.
Key insightUsers understood the action faster than they understood the explanation.
Design changeReplace the text-heavy tutorial with a short visual: Open job → Tap placED. → Review & save.

Task 3

Choose a resume & apply

“Open the job you’ve saved, choose which resume to send, apply on the portal, then make sure the app knows.”
What I wanted to learnCan users choose the appropriate resume and understand how the application status is updated?
Flow Journey→Job detail→ Resume choice→Application portal→ Applied
V1 screens
TimeUse the recorded timing for each participant where a reliable endpoint exists.
What I observed
  • Most users completed the main task without major difficulty.
  • 4 users skipped the Resume V5 checkbox.
  • Most users who noticed V5 asked what “V5” meant.
  • Some users explored the resume comparison screens.
Error / frictionThe resume version label was unfamiliar. Users understood the resume choice better than the version naming.
Key insightThe recommendation was useful, but the system terminology was not immediately clear.
Design changeShow the purpose first: “UX / Product Resume - V5 · Updated 2 Aug - 82% match.”

Task 4

Trust an automatic update

“A notification says Adobe shortlisted you. Open it and decide whether you believe it.”
What I wanted to learnWill users understand and verify an automatically detected status change?
Flow Notification→Job detail→ Why this changed→Confirm / Reject
V1 screens
TimeUse observed timings only; do not estimate missing endpoints.
What I observed
  • Almost all 10 users skipped “How did we know? →”.
  • Only 2 users opened the reasoning.
  • Those 2 users were concerned about what would happen if they confirmed the status and later needed to change it.
Error / frictionImportant trust information looked optional, and users were not sure whether an automatic status could be changed later.
Key insightUsers do not necessarily reject automation. They want to understand it and remain in control.
Design changeShow the source and reasoning directly in the confirmation flow, and clearly say the status can be changed later.

Task 5

Resolve a schedule clash

“Two things clash on Friday morning. Sort it out.”
What I wanted to learnCan users understand the conflict, choose what should move and review the generated communication?
Flow Home alert→Calendar→ Choose which moves→Draft request→ Send
V1 screens
TimeUse observed timings only. Do not estimate missing endpoints.
What I observed
  • Users understood the schedule clash.
  • Users could identify the overlapping placement activities.
  • Most users asked whether they could edit the generated email before sending it.
Error / frictionUsers wanted control over the message before it was sent.
Key insightUsers are comfortable letting placED. prepare the action, but they want the final say.
Design changeUse Draft → Review → Edit → Send instead of sending immediately.

Cross-task findings

Looking across the five tasks, the observations point to four larger patterns.

FindingEvidenceDesign response
Users want to explore before setupStudent Passport was often skipped.Reduce early setup and introduce profile completion when useful.
Use language users understandUsers were confused by Student Passport and “V5”.Explain the purpose first; keep system labels secondary.
Important information should not look optionalAlmost all 10 users skipped “How did we know?”.Bring important reasoning into the main interaction.
Automate the work, not the decisionUsers wanted to change status and edit the email.Use Suggest → Review → Confirm.

What worked

  • Most users could complete the main application flow.
  • All of them agreed to give their email access to the application. (Important)
  • Users understood the schedule clash.
  • Email connection was generally understandable.
  • Users were open to automatic status updates; their concern was mainly verification and control.
