Overview

Before designing anything, I wanted to understand how campus placement actually runs: where information reaches students, how they apply, and where things fall through the cracks. I interviewed students from 13 colleges across India and spoke to a placement cell.

The problem

How might we help students and job seekers organise and track their job applications effectively?
75–80 lakhbachelor’s graduates in India every year
?Why isn’t the current ecosystem already solving this?

Goal: understand the end-to-end campus placement experience of students, and identify the most significant challenges they face while navigating multiple recruitment platforms and processes.

Who I looked at

Early-career job seekers: final-year students, campus placement, off-campus applicants, fresh graduates, internship seekers, and young professionals changing jobs. I started with three groups:

Students

Questions I asked

Students

  1. Can you walk me through your placement journey so far?
  2. What platforms, apps or channels do you use during placement? (Superset, POD, WhatsApp…)
  3. Tell me about the last company you applied to. What steps did you go through?
  4. What is the most difficult or frustrating part of placement for you, and of the placement platform? Why?
  5. How do you currently keep track of your applications, deadlines, interviews and documents?
  6. Have you ever forgotten or missed something important during placement? What happened?
  7. Is there anything about the placement platform that causes you stress or feels overwhelming?
  8. Did you use the placement platform on mobile?

Placement cell

  1. Can you walk me through the placement process from the day a company contacts the college until offers are released?
  2. How do you usually communicate updates to students?
  3. Why was WhatsApp chosen over other platforms?
  4. Which information do you have to send repeatedly?
  5. Which tasks could you automate if you had the right tool?

A swimlane of one drive (POD college), from first contact to result.

The placement cell is very small compared to the number of students in a college. WhatsApp wins when the timeline is short, so the same update goes out on POD, WhatsApp and email. Automate: read Gmail → update the status, rather than the cell posting it three times.

Key insights

InsightEvidenceWhat it means for placED.
There is no single source of truth8 of 13 colleges had no platform. Even with one, updates still came over WhatsApp, Gmail, Outlook and Google Forms.Bring everything into one record the student owns.
Status is invisible“No status” at MIT, Presidency, VIT, SPA, SPPT and Amity. Students didn’t know if they were selected.Show every application’s current stage at a glance.
Updates arrive in the wrong placeUnread college Outlook, buried WhatsApp messages, calls from unknown numbers, no notifications.Read the inbox students apply from and update status automatically.
Students re-enter the same detailsProfile again on careers pages, platform + Google form, a new form per company.Capture a posting once; never re-type.
Resumes are hard to manageCan’t delete old resumes; tailoring per company.A resume library that remembers what went where.
Platforms don’t work on the phoneNot usable on mobile at Presidency, VIT and NIFT; session timeouts at Amity.Mobile first.

Also heard: overlapping interview timings (HR College) and test days that take the whole day (National Education). Both point to a calendar that flags clashes.

Ideation: two surfaces

The ideas split naturally between where students apply (the browser) and where they check in (the phone).

Web plugin

Where applying happens

  • Resume versions
  • Resume recommendations
  • Autofill forms from the resume
  • Scans the job page and stores the JD, dates and other details

Mobile app

Where updates land

  • View every application and its details
  • Resumes
  • Lock-screen widget for deadlines and other updates
  • Link Gmail, read updates from POD, LinkedIn and Naukri, and notify me
  • AI chatbot
Idea · exploredWhat if the plugin converts a Figma resume into an ATS-checked resume?

Whiteboarding the first flow

I sketched the mobile app’s first-run flow:

OnboardingDownload app→Sign up / log in (Gmail OAuth)→Connect Gmail for updates→Fill Student Passport→Prompt: install the browser plugin→Home: “No active applications yet!”
ThenUpdates are fetched from Gmail, and new jobs arrive from the browser plugin.

Questions I had to answer before going further:

Why not just use Notion or Sheets?Because here the status updates itself, and your notes stay yours.
Will students open the app, or just read notifications?Most students: notifications only, glance and go. An active subset opens the app for the resume centre and an AI copilot.

The Student Passport step from this sketch later turned out to be the most confusing part of onboarding in usability testing.

From research to design

The placement cell is stretched thin, so placED. can’t depend on them changing how they work. The app has to work around the mess students already live in.

1 · CaptureSee a job posting anywhere, tap the floating widget, and the app reads the details off the screen.
2 · TrackConnect the email you use to apply. The app reads placement mail and updates the status when a company replies.
3 · DecideThe app suggests which resume to send, flags interview clashes, and shows what needs attention today.
The student confirms things. They don’t maintain things.

These ideas were then tested in five task flows. See the test plan and the usability testing analysis.
