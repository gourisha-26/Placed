The problem I started with

Students don’t struggle to apply for jobs. They struggle to keep track of them.

Placement information is spread across the college portal, WhatsApp groups, Gmail and company websites. The student holds it all together: re-typing the same details, losing track of which resume went where, and missing updates that arrive in the wrong place at the wrong time.

Assumption to testMost tracking apps ask the student to maintain a list. That only works with the discipline to keep it updated during the busiest month of the year.

What I wanted to learn

  • Where does placement information reach students, and which channel do they trust most?
  • How do students track applications today, and do they keep it up?
  • How many resume versions do they manage, and do they remember which went where?
  • What have they missed: deadlines, replies, interview clashes?
  • How comfortable are they giving an app access to their email?

Who I spoke to

Final-year students

Students applying through campus placements now, or about to. They described their day-to-day process and what goes wrong.

Placement cell and faculty

They run the drives. They explained how information is sent out and where students most often drop the ball.

Interview questions I asked

  • Walk me through the last job you applied for. Where did you first hear about it?
  • How do you keep track of everything you’ve applied to?
  • Have you ever missed a deadline, update or interview? What happened?
  • How many versions of your resume do you have? How do you pick one?
  • Would you let an app read your placement email? What would make you say no?

Key insights

Across the interviews, the themes pointed to a few larger patterns.

InsightEvidenceWhat it means for placED.
The student is the system—The record should build itself, not rely on manual upkeep.
Manual tracking doesn’t survive placement season—Capture jobs from wherever they’re seen, in one tap.
Email is where the truth arrives—Read placement mail and update the status when a company replies.
Resume choice is guesswork—Suggest a resume and remember where each version went.

From research to design

The research shaped the three ideas behind placED.:

1 · CaptureSee a job posting anywhere, tap the floating widget, and the app reads the details off the screen.
2 · TrackConnect the email you use to apply. The app reads placement mail and updates the status when a company replies.
3 · DecideThe app suggests which resume to send, flags interview clashes, and shows what needs attention today.
The student confirms things. They don’t maintain things.

These ideas were then tested in five task flows. See the test plan and the usability testing analysis.
